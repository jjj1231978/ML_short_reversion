# Research Quality Review: ML Short-Term Mean Reversion Backtest

## Must-fix

1. **Missing per-member SHAP justification for ensemble underperformance** — The draft states "the ensemble (1.061 IR) underperforms RandomForest (1.139 IR)" and notes MLP "is learning macro-hedging rather than idiosyncratic return prediction," but provides no evidence from the research pack that MLP's feature set (GOLD_HMM_TRANS_P, OIL_HMM_TRANS_P, OIL_R5, etc.) is actually in the training feature inventory. The research pack lists `phase1_factor_inventory` as 13 factors—none of which include HMM states or commodity betas. These features (BETA_VIX, OIL_R5, GOLD_HMM_TRANS_P, etc.) appear in `shap_top_features` but are **not defined in `factor_inventory`**. Either they are missing from the inventory (a data error in the pack), or the draft is reporting SHAP features that don't exist in the stated factor set. This is a critical inconsistency: you cannot rank features in SHAP that were not in the input. Clarify what the actual training feature set is, or flag this as a data-integrity issue.

2. **Invented factor names in SHAP analysis** — The draft and research pack both cite SHAP rankings for features like "BETA_VIX", "OIL_R5", "GOLD_HMM_TRANS_P", "EURUSD_HMM_TRANS_P", "DXY_TREND_UP", "GOLD_HMM_S1_P", "COPPER_HMM_TRANS_P", "OIL_HMM_S0_P", "GOLD_HMM_S0_P" in the `shap_top_features_per_model` tables. **None of these appear in `phase1_factor_inventory`**, which lists only 13 factors: R1W, IREV1W, RSI5D, RSI14, R3M1M, R12M1M, VOL6M, BETA6M, PE, PB, ROE, GPOA, UPDOWN1W_RATINGS. The research pack's `shap_top_features` section claims R1W, SIZE, PSALES, IMOM12M1M, BETA_VIX, OIL_R5, R12M1M, BETA6M, VOL12M, IVOL12M are the top-10, but SIZE, PSALES, IMOM12M1M, BETA_VIX, OIL_R5, VOL12M, IVOL12M are **not in the declared 13-factor inventory**. This is a fundamental data integrity problem. The draft must either (a) correct the factor inventory to include all features actually used in training, or (b) flag that the research pack's SHAP data is inconsistent with its declared schema and cannot be trusted. Until this is resolved, the entire SHAP-based validation is unreliable.

3. **Quintile-spread validation marked "No data" but required by checklist** — The draft's Objective section lists "Quintile spread: Is long-short performance monotonic across performance quintiles?" as a key validation check (item 5), notes it is evaluable (marked as item 5 in "Items evaluable from this run"), yet in the Results section concludes "Per-quintile returns are not reported in the research pack. Cannot evaluate." This is a contradiction. Either the check is not evaluable (move it to "not evaluable"), or the data exists somewhere and should be cited. The research pack does not provide per-quintile return tables in `metrics_summary`, `model_comparison`, or elsewhere visible in the JSON. However, the paper (page 1, "Global long/short performance by market performance quintile") shows 13.4%, 6.9%, 6.8%, 8.3%, 14.4% for "Highest, High, Average, Low, Lowest" quintiles, which appears non-monotonic (Highest and Lowest are both high). The draft should either cite the paper's quintile performance and compare to this run's results (if available), or formally acknowledge that this run did not calculate per-quintile performance and therefore cannot validate this item.

4. **R1W SHAP claim contradicts per-model rankings** — The draft states "All but MLP rank R1W first, confirming the paper's claim," but the research pack shows MLP's top-10 has R1W **not listed at all**. The MLP top-10 in `shap_top_features_per_model.mlp` starts with OIL_R5, GOLD_HMM_TRANS_P, OIL_HMM_TRANS_P, GOLD_HMM_S1_P, PSALES, etc. R1W does not appear. The draft's statement "All but MLP rank R1W first" is factually correct, but the characterization of this as "confirming the paper's claim" is misleading—if 1 of 4 ensemble members (MLP) ignores the mean-reversion signal entirely and focuses on commodity regimes, the ensemble's ability to reliably discover the signal is weaker than the language suggests. Revise to note: "XGBoost, LightGBM, and RandomForest all rank R1W first; MLP diverges sharply and does not include R1W in its top-10, suggesting instability in the discovery of the mean-reversion signal across architectures."

5. **Ensemble combination method not justified against paper** — The draft states the ensemble uses "rank-mean" combination but provides no evidence that the paper uses the same method. The paper says "our ML framework" and "ensemble" but does not specify combination method. The draft then claims "The ensemble (1.061 IR) underperforms RandomForest (1.139 IR) by 0.078 IR points, suggesting the rank-mean combination is diluting the best performer." This is unsupported inference: rank-mean may be a poor choice, or the paper may use a superior method (e.g., inverse-volatility weighting, Bayesian model averaging). The draft should state explicitly: "The paper does not disclose its ensemble combination method. This run uses rank-mean, which may not match the paper's approach. If the paper uses a more sophisticated method, that could partially explain the IR gap." Then cite whether this gap is quantifiable (e.g., "Switching to inverse-vol weighting yielded X% IR improvement in exploratory testing").

6. **Paper's baseline returns misquoted or misinterpreted** — The draft's Motivation section states: "The paper reports that its ML model achieves **9.7% annualized return at 6.3% volatility (1.6 IR) net of costs**, beating both the basic reversal baseline (5.3% return, 5.2% vol, 1.01 IR)..." However, the paper (page 10, table "Risk and return statistics (long/short, 2006–2025)") shows **Global:** ML Long/Short 9.7% return, 6.3% vol, 1.6 IR; **US:** 8.6% return, 8.5% vol, 1.0 IR. The paper also shows (page 10) a regional comparison table. The draft's 5.3% return and 5.2% vol appear to be from a US-only subset ("US" row in the paper's table), not the "basic reversal baseline" (which the paper reports separately on page 5–6). Quote the paper's table directly: page 5, "Annualised risk and return statistics (2006-2025)" shows "Basic Price Reversals: Long/Short Long 14.9%, Short 3.3%, vol 6.1% / 4.9%, IR 2.44 / 0.67." The draft's baseline numbers are not clearly sourced from the paper; clarify which baseline is being compared or quote the paper's table.

7. **"Weaker proxy" claim for UPDOWN1W_RATINGS unsupported by controlled test** — The draft asserts: "If analyst-rating revisions are a weak proxy for EPS consensus shifts, the ensemble will rank it lower, and the headline IR will suffer." The observation confirms the ranking is lower (UPDOWN1W_RATINGS outside top-10), but the draft does not provide a controlled ablation test (e.g., "Removing UPDOWN1W_RATINGS from the feature set and retraining yielded X% IR change"). Without such evidence, the causal claim "ranking it lower → IR suffers" is speculative. Revise to: "UPDOWN1W_RATINGS does not appear in the ensemble's top-10 SHAP features, consistent with analyst-rating revisions being a weaker alpha driver than EPS-consensus revisions. A controlled feature-ablation study would quantify the IR loss from this substitution; absent such analysis, the contribution is inferred but unquantified."

8. **Alpha decay non-monotonicity not explained** — The Results section reports: "The pattern is non-monotonic (lag 2 bounces back to 8.94%)," and the table shows 0d: 12.54%, 1d: 7.84%, 2d: 8.94%, 3d: 6.52%, 4d: 6.07%. The draft attributes this to "noise or a multi-day rebalancing cycle artifact" without evidence. A rebalancing-cycle artifact would create a regular, predictable pattern; the 1d→2d bounce does not align with a weekly cycle (which would peak at day 7). The draft should either (a) investigate whether the bounce correlates with a specific calendar pattern (e.g., earnings announcement concentration on certain weekdays), or (b) flag it as an unexplained variance and caution against over-interpreting single lag-point returns. Alternatively, cite whether this pattern appears in the paper's own alpha-decay analysis (the paper shows 0d: 14.9%, 1d: 11.6%, 2d: 9.5%, 3d: 8.1%, 4d: 6.8%—a smooth decay), and note that this run's non-monotonicity is a deviation requiring explanation.

9. **Paper's earnings data release pattern and weekday effect not directly compared** — The draft states "All days show much higher returns (22–35%) than the 1d-lag headline (7.84%), because the weekday-effect analysis uses the no-lag scenario." This is a critical methodological point: the paper's weekday analysis (page 8) uses 1-day lag (stated: "we calculated the performance of the portfolio when rebalanced weekly using data from different weekdays" with "1-day lag"). This run's weekday table does not specify lag. The draft then notes "The paper does not provide numerical weekday returns; it shows only a bar chart" (true for pages 8 charts). But the paper does provide text: "Thursday performs best, Monday worse." The bar chart y-axes show return percentages but are unlabeled in your text. To validate the weekday effect, directly compare the paper's ordering (Thursday > Monday) and regression structure (% data releases by day) to this run's ordering and returns. Currently, the draft states the pattern is "qualitatively aligned" without numbers from either source. Cite both the paper's weekday ordering and this run's percentages, and test whether they are statistically significantly different.

10. **Survivorship-bias impact quantified only for UK/CA, not for paper** — The Comparability Caveats section states: "UK and Canada use current snapshots, not point-in-time membership, introducing survivorship bias." However, the paper also uses a current snapshot for UK and Japan ("Our universe excludes financial stocks given many of our input factors are not available for financials," and "global developed market large/mid-cap" with no explicit statement of point-in-time membership for non-US regions). The paper's Appendix I does not clarify whether UK and Japan are point-in-time or snapshot. The draft should either (a) check the paper's data methodology (does it cite FactSet or another vendor that typically provides point-in-time?), or (b) state "The paper does not explicitly disclose whether non-US constituents are point-in-time or snapshot; assuming snapshot, the paper's global results are also survivorship-biased, limiting the comparability of this gap."

11. **Return cap of ±0.3 is not in the paper, but impact not quantified** — The draft acknowledges: "A weekly return cap of ±0.3 (30%) is applied post-cost. This reduces tail risk but also clips upside in high-vol weeks. The paper does not mention a return cap." However, no evidence is provided that the cap actually reduced this run's reported returns. If the cap is binding in any week, the 7.84% annualized return is artificially suppressed relative to the uncapped version. The draft should either (a) remove the cap and re-report the uncapped performance, or (b) quantify the return loss from the cap (e.g., "Without the cap, returns would have been X%; the cap reduced performance by Y bps"). Proceed without that data is acceptable if acknowledged as a limitation, but the draft should not claim the run matches the paper's approach without noting this constraint.

## Should-fix

1. **Ensemble member selection not justified** — The draft includes MLP (a neural network), but MLP's top-10 SHAP features are almost entirely macro/commodity factors (OIL_R5, GOLD_HMM_TRANS_P, COPPER_HMM_TRANS_P, etc.), which are not in the declared factor inventory. If MLP is learning something orthogonal to the equity-factor-based approach, why include it in the ensemble? The draft notes "MLP diverges" but does not recommend removing it. A sentence like "MLP's focus on commodity regimes may provide diversification value during commodity-shock periods; however, the ensemble's underperformance vs. RandomForest suggests the trade-off is unfavorable" would justify the design choice or recommend revision.

2. **Training-window shortness not quantified against paper** — The draft states "The run's 260-week training window (5 years) is well below the paper's implicit 520-week (10-year) rolling window." However, the paper is not explicit about rolling-window length. The paper says "We kept the 10 year rolling training and 2 year rolling validation periods" on page 4. The draft correctly identifies this, but does not estimate the impact. A statement like "Shorter training windows typically increase overfitting risk and reduce long-memory signal detection (e.g., for mean reversion, which may depend on multi-year regime stability). We did not test whether extending the window to 520 weeks would improve IR; this remains an open question" would be more honest.

3. **"Regime-balanced sample" claim not supported** — The draft states: "The OOS period (2013–2026) emphasizes 2017–2021, a low-vol era where mean reversion is weaker... The paper's 1990–2024 window has more mean-reversion-friendly regimes." However, the paper's backtest window is 2006–2025 (stated repeatedly), not 1990–2024. The 1990–2024 reference is for a separate momentum back-test (page 2, "Annualised back-tested performance of US momentum strategies by holding period (1990-2024)"), not the main ML strategy. Correct: "The paper's 2006–2025 window includes the 2008–2009 GFC and 2020 COVID crash, both mean-reversion-friendly regimes. This run's OOS period (2013–2026) includes the low-vol 2017–2019 period, which may reduce average alpha. However, both windows span the same 2006–2013 base period, so the difference in regime exposure is limited to 2013–2025 vs 2025–2026."

4. **MLP feature set source not disclosed** — The draft reproduces MLP's top-10 SHAP features from the research pack without noting that these features (GOLD_HMM_TRANS_P, OIL_HMM_TRANS_P, COPPER_HMM_TRANS_P, DXY_TREND_UP, etc.) do not appear in the declared `phase1_factor_inventory`.
