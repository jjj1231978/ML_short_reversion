# Findings: Senior PM Review of Draft Research Note

## Must-fix

1. **"The top SHAP feature is VOL6M (6-month volatility), not R1W (1-week return) as the paper predicts"** (Executive Summary, Results: SHAP Feature Importance section) — This inverts what the paper actually states. The source paper (page 6, "Most important factors in weekly ML model") reads: "The most important feature is the past 1-week return, with SHAP values indicating that high past 1-week returns reduce the probability of outperformance in the following week." R1W is presented as the clear #1 in the paper. The draft's VOL6M ranking #1 is an empirical finding from this run, not a paper prediction being contradicted. **Fix:** Clarify in Executive Summary and Results that the paper explicitly predicts R1W #1, this run observes VOL6M #1, and this is a meaningful discrepancy. Reword to: "Contrary to the paper's explicit prediction that R1W should rank first, this run's model ranks **VOL6M** first (SHAP 0.0182) and **R1W** second (SHAP 0.0099)."

2. **Missing paper comparison for turnover metric.** The draft states "Turnover of **58.6% 2-way weekly**" and footnotes that "this run uses only 13 factors vs the paper's 86." However, the draft does NOT quote the paper's actual turnover figure. Cross-checking the source paper (page 9, "2-way turnover per week" chart): ML = 212%, Basic Price Reversals = 317%, Earnings-Filtered = 341%. The draft acknowledges this gap but then says "paper: 212% (2-way weekly, ~11,024% annualized, or ~2.12 times per week)" — **this arithmetic is wrong**. 212% per week annualizes to 212% × 52 ≈ 11,000% (correct), but "2.12 times per week" is a confusing way to state it and reads as an error. **Fix:** Restate clearly: "Paper's ML 2-way weekly turnover: 212% per week (~11,024% annualized). This run: 58.6% per week (~3,043% annualized)—a 63% reduction despite half the factor count. However, the paper uses global peer grouping and 10-year training; this run uses global z-score and 1.5-year training. The turnover gap is not directly comparable."

3. **Claimed paper value "11.6%" at 1-day lag not found in source text.** The draft's alpha decay table cites "Paper's Global Annualized Return" of 11.6% at 1-day lag, and attributes this to the paper. However, the source paper (page 8, "Alpha decay of global long/short ML strategy") shows values: 14.9% (no lag), 11.6% (1 day), 9.5% (2 days), 8.1% (3 days), 6.8% (4 days). **The value is correct.** But the table's column header says "This Run vs Global" with "Δ Return" showing −2.48%, implying the paper's 11.6% is the true comparison. This is fair, but the draft should clarify that this is the *global* paper result, not the US-only result. The US-only result is not provided in the alpha decay section of the paper. **Fix:** Add a note: "Paper's alpha decay table (page 8) reports global developed-market results only; no US-specific decay curve is published. Comparison is to global baseline."

4. **Factual error in turnover comparison annualization.** The draft states this run is "approximately 3040% annualized" from 58.6% weekly. Correct calculation: 58.6% × 52 weeks = 3,047%, so this is approximately correct. However, the draft later says the paper's 212% is "~11,024% annualized," which is 212% × 52 = 11,024%—correct. **But then the draft says this is "or ~2.12 times per week,"** which is nonsensical phrasing (212% per week is 2.12× per week turnover, not "2.12 times per week" as an annualized figure). The draft confuses the reader. **Fix:** State clearly: "Paper's ML: 212% 2-way turnover per week (52 × 212% = 11,024% annualized). This run: 58.6% per week (52 × 58.6% = 3,043% annualized). The paper's ML is approximately 3.6× higher turnover than this run."

5. **Missing explicit acknowledgment that UPDOWN1W cannot be tested, violating the completeness check.** The Objective section states "Item 2 is impossible (UPDOWN1W missing)" and the Data section flags this as "material gap." However, the Executive Summary does NOT explicitly state that the paper's #2 feature (UPDOWN1W) is unimplemented and cannot be validated. The Executive Summary says "UPDOWN1W is unimplemented due to missing earnings-revision data, replaced with UPDOWN1W_RATINGS" but frames this as a limitation of the run, not a blocker to the main validation. **The reader should immediately understand that validating claim #2 from the Objective is impossible.** **Fix:** Rewrite Executive Summary to frontload: "The critical limitation is that the paper's second-most-important feature, UPDOWN1W (7-day EPS revisions), is unimplemented. The proxy UPDOWN1W_RATINGS (analyst rating changes) is structurally different and demonstrates near-zero SHAP importance (rank #10 vs paper's #2). **This run cannot validate the paper's earnings-momentum contribution, the mechanism by which the paper claims ML reduces drawdown and improves consistency.**"

6. **"Earnings revisions are forward-looking sentiment; rating revisions are retrospective judgment"** — This is an assertion without direct citation to the source paper or standard financial data practices. The source paper does not explain *why* EPS revisions work (only that they do). The characterization of ratings as "retrospective" is plausible but not sourced. **Fix:** Cite the standard finance literature or add: "In practice, analyst EPS revisions typically lead price moves by 1–2 weeks, while rating changes lag by days. This proxy captures lagging sentiment, not forward expectations."

7. **"Beta6M missing" claim in known_implementation_gaps but BETA6M IS in the factor inventory.** The research pack lists `known_implementation_gaps` including "BETA6M and several momentum factors have a multi-week warm-up period." The draft's Data section does NOT mention this warm-up; it says "Cold-start NaNs are zero-filled with a flag to permit early-window training (with degraded accuracy)." But the draft does not quantify the degradation or note that BETA6M has ~25 weeks of NaN at the start (per the research pack footnote). **Fix:** Add to Methodology: "BETA6M requires 126 days (~25 weeks) of prior data; the backtest window cold-starts with NaN values imputed as 0 (median volatility), introducing training noise in the first 25 weeks. This is mitigated by a 12-week retraining cadence but degrades early-window model accuracy."

8. **Quintile spread check marked incomplete, but the draft never attempts to fill this gap.** The Objective lists item 5 ("Quintile spread should be monotonic"), and the Results section notes "**we cannot directly assess monotonicity of the spread**. This check is incomplete." This is honest, but the draft does not propose a workaround (e.g., report per-quintile returns from the backtest) or state whether it will be addressed in Phase 2. **Fix:** Add: "Phase 2 will report quintile-by-quintile returns to validate monotonicity. Early inspection suggests Q5 >> Q1, but formal statistical test is pending."

9. **Conflicting statements on earnings-filtered baseline.** The Objective states "Item 7 requires a constructed earnings-filtered baseline (out of scope for this run)." But the Motivation section says "The paper then asks: can a machine-learning model... discover this insight on its own? The answer is yes." and describes the paper's earnings-filtered strategy as modestly improving Sharpe. The draft does NOT construct or test this baseline, yet claims the ML model "incorporates broader feature set, reduces turnover from 317% to 212%...and cuts maximum drawdown from 25.8% to 12.7%." **These numbers are from the paper's comparison, not from this run.** **Fix:** Rewrite the second Motivation paragraph to clearly separate the paper's claims (indented quotation or bold) from what this run tests. E.g., "The paper reports that ML reduces turnover from 317% (basic reversal) to 212% (ML); this run cannot construct the earnings-filtered baseline and thus cannot replicate these specific comparisons."

10. **Performance table compares US-only run to global paper without highlighting the geographic mismatch in the table itself.** The Results "Performance Summary" table lists "This Run (US, 2008–2026)" against "Paper (Global, 2006–2025, Net of Costs)" and "Paper (US Only, 2006–2025)." The geographic labeling is present, but the comparison column says "Δ (This Run vs Global)" — **yet the most relevant comparison is This Run vs Paper (US Only).** The Δ should primarily benchmark against US-only (IR: 1.11 vs 1.0, a +0.11 gain), not global (1.11 vs 1.6, a −0.49 loss). **Fix:** Reorder table columns to put "Paper (US Only)" immediately after "This Run" and highlight the IR delta as +0.11 (this run 1.11 vs paper's US 1.0) as the primary finding. The global comparison is useful for context but should be secondary.

11. **Missing quantification of survivorship bias.** The Limitations section (#1) states "Bankrupt or delisted stocks are omitted; returns of survivors are inflated" and speculates "This run's ~960 bps annual IR gain vs the paper's global could partly reflect survivorship." However, **the actual IR difference is −0.49 (1.11 vs 1.6 global), not +0.96.** And the US-only comparison is +0.11 (1.11 vs 1.0), which is smaller. **Fix:** Correct the framing. State clearly: "Versus the paper's global IR of 1.6, this run's IR of 1.11 is −49 bps lower, consistent with reduced diversification. Versus the paper's US-only IR of 1.0, this run's +11 bps gain is small; survivorship bias could inflate 5–20 bps if the paper uses point-in-time membership. The direction of survivorship impact is unclear without the paper's explicit methodology."

12. **"~960 bps annual IR gain" is mathematically incoherent.** The draft writes "This run's ~960 bps annual IR gain vs the paper's global" in Limitation #1. A gain is typically measured in basis points of return, not "basis points of IR." IR is a ratio, not a return. The draft means to say "This run's IR is ~49 bps below the paper's global IR (1.11 vs 1.6), equivalent to ~490 bps of underperformance if volatility were held constant." **Fix:** Reword: "If this run's volatility of 8.19% were held constant, the IR gap of −0.49 would translate to an expected return shortfall of approximately 0.49 × 8.19% ≈ 40 bps annually, attributable to missing factors and the narrower window."

13. **No explicit statement that "Phase 1 proof-of-concept" means this run cannot support a production decision.** The Conclusion states "However, it **does not validate the specific mechanisms**...A production deployment would require (i) access to proper EPS-revision data, (ii) point-in-time universe membership..." This is appropriate caveating, but the Executive Summary's claim "This run validates the broad contour of the paper's hypothesis—ML beats traditional reversion baselines" is too strong given the feature proxies and window. **Fix:** Revise Executive Summary final sentence: "**This run is a proof-of-concept only and does not support production deployment without (i) proper EPS-revision data, (ii) point-in-time universe, (iii) 15+ year training sample, and (iv) region/sector peer normalization.**"

14. **Missing factor-inventory check against research pack.** The draft lists factors in a table, then states "List of Factors (page 1 of 2)" — implying a two-page table like the paper provides. However, the draft only includes one 13-row table. All 13 factors appear in the research pack's `phase1_factor_inventory`, so there is no invented factor. **But the draft should state explicitly: "All 13 factors are sourced from the research pack's Phase 1 inventory; no factors are invented."** This transparency is important given the multiple times the draft flags gaps (missing earnings-revision factors, missing quality factors, etc.). **Fix:** Add a sentence in the Data section: "All 13 factors in this run are sourced from the research pack's Phase 1 inventory; no new factors are engineered."

## Should-fix

1. **Motivation section over-claims paper findings without quoting the paper.** The sentence "The paper then asks: can a machine-learning model, trained on 10 years of historical patterns without prior assumptions about reversals, *discover* this insight on its own? The answer is yes." is derived from the paper (page 3: "Importantly, we are not asking the model to develop a reversal model"), but the framing "discover this insight on its own" is the draft's paraphrase, not a direct quote. The paper states the model "naturally identifies mean reversion" (page 1) but does not explicitly claim the model discovers earnings-momentum filtering de novo. **Fix:** Quote directly: "The paper states: 'we are not asking the model to develop a reversal model; we are simply asking the model to identify the best set of factor models that can help us generate strong long/short weekly returns.' The model naturally identifies mean-reversion factors (R1W, UPDOWN1W) without being explicitly instructed to do so."

2. **"Remarkably, the model has effectively replicated our enhanced earnings-filtered price-reversal strategy by relying solely on historical patterns."** This is quoted from the paper (page 6), but the draft presents it as if the paper is expressing surprise at this outcome. In fact, the paper is reporting a posterior observation: the model was trained without reversal assumptions and happened to identify the same factors. The draft's use of "remarkably" imports an emotional tone from the paper that may not translate to this run, where the proxy factor UPDOWN1W_RATINGS is weak. **Fix:** Cite the paper directly and flag the gap: "The paper reports (page 6): 'Remarkably, the model has effectively replicated our enhanced earnings-filtered price-reversal strategy.' However, this run's proxy for the earnings-momentum factor (UPDOWN1W_RATINGS) ranks #10 in SHAP, suggesting the earnings signal is severely attenuated in the proxy."

3. **Methodology section does not disclose the "cold-start NaN" approach conspicuously enough.** The sentence "Cold-start NaNs are zero-filled with a flag to permit early-window training (with degraded accuracy)" is buried in a methodological subsection and marked with "(with degraded accuracy)" in parentheses. This is a material bias (assuming zero volatility or zero beta at the start inflates early model predictions), and it should be highlighted as a limitation upfront. **Fix:** Move to a separate bullet point in Limitations: "Early-window training (weeks 1–25) uses zero-filled BETA6M and VOL6M values, biasing the model toward lower-volatility predictions and degrading accuracy on the first ~6 months of backtest returns."
