4. **Weekday effect "PARTIAL FAILURE" claim is not supported by paper's actual predictions.**
   - *Quoted:* "Validation check 4: ✗ PARTIAL FAILURE. The paper predicts Thursday > Monday, and our data confirm Monday is the weakest (19.1% return, 2.74 IR). However, Friday is the clear winner (37.8% return, 3.64 IR), not Thursday (28.7% return, 2.73 IR)."
   - *Issue:* The paper's weekday analysis (page 8, "Weekday effects") shows a chart "Global Long/Short basic price-reversal strategy based on the day we calculate the signal (1-day lag)" comparing Monday through Friday performance. The source text states: "There seems to be a strong weekday effect, consistent between the ML and traditional price reversal strategies (Thursday performs best, Monday worse)." **The paper does NOT predict that Thursday is universally best; it observes that in the 2006–2025 global sample, Thursday outperforms Monday.** Our Friday > Thursday finding is not a "failure" of the paper's prediction; it's a divergence in regime. The paper also notes: "Interestingly, the ML model is far more consistent across rebalancing days, and although still affected by seasonality, it is to a far lesser extent." The draft interprets this as a binary prediction (Thursday will always win) rather than an empirical observation (Thursday won in the paper's sample). Additionally, the draft attributes the paper's observation to "concentration of US economic data releases (28% of releases on Thursday vs. 8% on Monday, per the paper's chart)", but the paper's actual chart (page 8, bottom-right) shows: Monday 8%, Tuesday 17%, Wednesday 23%, **Thursday 28%, Friday 24%.** The distribution does *not* explain Thursday >> Friday; in fact, Friday has almost as many releases. The draft has misread the paper's causal mechanism.
   - *Suggested fix:* Revise to: "Validation check 4: ✗ INCONCLUSIVE. The paper observes Thursday outperforms Monday in the 2006–2025 global sample, which our data confirm (19.1% Monday vs. 28.7% Thursday). However, our Friday return (37.8%) exceeds both, suggesting either: (a) regime shift in earnings/data release timing post-2025, (b) absence of earnings-revision factors (UPDOWN1W) altering the timing of reversion captures, or (c) a data artifact of the US-only sample. The paper's causal attribution to economic data releases does not fully explain the pattern (Thursday 28%, Friday 24% of releases are nearly equal), leaving the mechanism ambiguous."

7. **"Regime luck" and "survivorship bias" claims lack quantification.**
   - *Quoted:* "The elevated returns and IR are better understood as favorable regime capture and survivorship bias than as evidence of the paper's claimed ML-driven enhancement over traditional strategies."
   - *Issue:* The draft asserts survivorship bias inflates returns by "~50–100 bps annually (per academic literature)" but does not cite a specific source and does not quantify survivorship bias in this specific run. The research pack does not provide a survival-adjusted backtest. The draft also states the 3-year window captures "a single post-COVID regime with structural mean-reversion tailwinds" but does not decompose the 29% return into regime factors (e.g., how much is due to vol normalization vs. model skill?). Without this decomposition, "regime luck" is an unsupported narrative.
   - *Suggested fix:* (A) Cite a specific academic paper (e.g., Blitz et al., or Arnott et al. on survivorship bias) with a ballpark figure. (B) Compute a point-in-time backtest excluding delisted/downgraded stocks to estimate actual survivorship bias. (C) Decompose returns: plot rolling-window IR over the 3 years to show if performance is stable or concentrated in certain sub-periods. (D) Perform a factor attribution: regress portfolio returns on SPY returns, VIX changes, and dollar index changes to isolate regime-specific alpha.

9. **Earnings data lag limitation is mentioned but not assessed in results.**
   - *Quoted:* Section "Limitations" #6: "SimFin TTM data has a multi-day publication lag; earnings surprises at the close may not be reflected in the feature matrix until the next update. This introduces a look-ahead bias if we are using current earnings in our Friday predictions."
   - *Issue:* This is a critical limitation but the draft does not quantify its impact. If SimFin data lags by 1–3 days, then feature values on Thursday/Friday may be stale earnings announcements, introducing a systematic time-decay in earnings-related signals. This could explain why Friday (when earnings are most stale) outperforms Thursday (when more recent earnings are available). The draft should test this by restricting the backtest to pre-earnings-announcement weeks and re-running.
   - *Suggested fix:* Add a note: "Phase 2 analysis should assess the impact of earnings data lag by: (A) tracking the publication date of each SimFin earnings update, (B) restricting the backtest to weeks with no earnings announcements >2 days stale, and (C) comparing results to the full-sample backtest to estimate bias magnitude."
