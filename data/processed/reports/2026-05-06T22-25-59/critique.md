# Editorial Review: "Testing ML-Enhanced Mean-Reversion on US Large-Cap Equities"

---

## Must-fix

1. **Headline metrics phantom claim** — "Headline performance metrics are not yet produced" (Executive Summary, first paragraph) yet the Executive Summary immediately asserts "the factor pipeline confirms the R1W (1-week return) factor is constructed and available as a candidate feature" and later states "preliminary validation shows promise on structural checks." This is misleading: confirming a factor exists is not a structural validation of the model's claims. A factor being "available" says nothing about whether it will rank first in SHAP or deliver positive alpha. Rephrase to: "We have not yet run the backtest loop; therefore we cannot report returns, volatility, or information ratio. The following section documents what is implemented and what remains."

2. **Invented metric: "Headline performance metrics are not yet produced" used as a narrative device** — The draft uses this phrase as if it were a data point ("Headline performance metrics are not yet produced, preventing direct comparison..."). This is accurate but then immediately softens it with "preliminary validation shows promise," which oversells the value of having a factor in inventory. Remove the softening. The honest statement is: "The backtest has not run; we cannot validate any of the paper's quantitative claims. We have only confirmed factor availability."

3. **Unsupported causal claim on earnings revisions** — "Without it, the Phase 1 model is training on price reversals and technicals in isolation, stripping out the behavioral insight that prices overreact more when earnings surprises are absent." This causal framing ("stripping out") is not supported by the data—the paper shows UPDOWN1W ranks #2, but the paper does not isolate the counterfactual performance of the model without UPDOWN1W. The draft assumes absence of earnings data will reduce alpha by "1–2 information ratio points" (Limitations section) but cites only indirect evidence ("70% correlation"). Rephrase to: "The paper's SHAP analysis ranks UPDOWN1W second, suggesting earnings momentum is material. Without it, we cannot test whether the model's performance degrades or whether other factors compensate."

4. **Misquoted paper figure on earnings-filtered correlation** — The draft states: "based on the paper's finding that earnings-filtered reversal alone recovers 70% correlation with the full ML model." This figure does not appear in `source_paper_full_text`. Search of the paper shows correlation tables between strategies (ML, Basic Price Reversals, Earnings Filtered Price Reversals) but no "70% correlation with full ML model" statement. The table on page 6 shows correlations: ML vs Earnings Filtered = 70%, but this is correlation *between two strategies*, not "recovery of correlation with the full model." Correct: "The paper shows 70% correlation between the ML strategy and the earnings-filtered reversal strategy (page 6), suggesting partial overlap in logic."

5. **Missing required section: Methodology gaps acknowledgment** — The Methodology section describes what *should* happen (neutralization, BETA6M wiring, ADV filtering) but buries the fact that these are not implemented. The phrase "not wired into main.py" appears, but only in parenthetical asides. A reader might finish Methodology thinking the pipeline matches the paper. Add a mandatory subsection: **"Implementation Gaps vs. Paper Specification"** that lists, in tabular form, each paper requirement and its Phase 1 status (Implemented / Placeholder / Not Wired / Phase 2 Pending). This belongs immediately after the "Missing-Data Handling" paragraph.

6. **Survivorship bias magnitude unsourced** — "The magnitude of this bias is typically 50–100 basis points per year in alpha studies, a material but not dominant effect." This is stated as fact but is not cited from the research pack, the paper, or a peer-reviewed source. The phrase "typically" is a red flag. Either cite the source or replace with: "Survivorship bias is expected to inflate returns, though the magnitude is difficult to quantify without point-in-time membership data. Comparable studies suggest 50–100bp, but this is an estimate pending detailed analysis."

7. **Unfounded claim on SHAP interpretation under non-neutralized features** — "Once neutralization is wired, the SHAP ranks may shift. The headline metrics (return, volatility, IR) should be less sensitive..." This assumes SHAP ranks are more sensitive than headline metrics, but the research pack does not provide evidence for this ordering. Rephrase to: "Neutralization affects feature collinearity and potentially SHAP ranks; the direction and magnitude of these changes is uncertain without empirical testing."

8. **Missing section: Validation Checklist Results** — The Objective section itemizes "eight testable claims" derived from the paper. The Results section should have a subsection **"Validation Checklist Status"** explicitly scoring each claim (Pass / Fail / Not Yet Computed). Currently, the checklist is listed in Objective but never revisited against actual results. Add a table with columns: [Claim #] [Claim] [Status] [Evidence / Gap]. This is essential for transparency.

9. **Alpha decay interpretation without data** — The draft presents the paper's alpha decay path (14.9% → 11.6% → 9.5% → 8.1% → 6.8%) and states "We expect Phase 1 to show similar decay dynamics if the one-week signal is robust; deviations from the paper's path would indicate regime dependence or factor instability." This is reasonable but frames the expectations as a null hypothesis without acknowledging that 3 years of data will not be sufficient to distinguish true regime dependence from noise. Rephrase: "If Phase 1 alpha decay deviates significantly from the paper's path, it could indicate (a) regime-specific effects in 2023–2026, (b) noise due to short sample length, or (c) structural differences in the US-only universe. Statistical power to discriminate (a) from (b) is limited."

10. **Unsupported claim on market consolidation impact** — "The 2023–2026 window also captures a period of consolidation in the S&P 500 (index concentration at mega-cap tech peaked in late 2024), which mechanically reduces the benefit of diversification across deciles and sectors." This is asserted but not quantified. How much does concentration reduce diversification benefit? Does this increase or decrease mean-reversion alpha? The relationship is not obvious. Either cite evidence or remove the claim. If retained, add: "We hypothesize that sector concentration reduces diversification; the net effect on mean-reversion alpha is unclear and will be evaluated post-backtest."

11. **Borrow-cost claim unsourced** — "Large-cap US short-selling is liquid and cheap (typically 2–10bp annually)." This is plausible but not from the research pack or paper. If citing industry convention, note it: "According to industry convention, US large-cap short borrow costs range 2–10bp annually, though this is not detailed in the paper." The phrase "typically" is weak without a citation.

12. **Missing explicit statement on which paper claims CANNOT be tested in Phase 1** — The draft lists findings that "cannot yet be tested" but does not clearly state which are *impossible* to test vs. *not yet computed*. For example:
    - UPDOWN1W ranking: *impossible* (data missing)
    - Weekday effects: *not yet computed* (data available, diagnostic raises NotImplementedError)
    - R1W ranking: *not yet computed* (data available)
    
    Add a subsection: **"Untestable vs. Deferred Claims"** that separates these categories. Example: "The following paper claims cannot be tested without Phase 2 data work: (a) UPDOWN1W SHAP rank (earnings revisions absent), (b) specific SHAP magnitudes (model not yet trained). The following can be tested once the backtest loop completes: (c) R1W SHAP rank, (d) alpha decay, (e) weekday effects."

## Should-fix

1. **Comparability Caveats subsection placement** — The "Comparability Caveats" is currently in the Results section, which is correct. However, the Executive Summary should include the *headline* comparability caveat (the 3-year vs 19-year and US-only vs global facts) more prominently. Currently, the Exec Summary notes "five critical comparability caveats" but does not quantify the impact on expected IR. Revise the Exec Summary to include a sentence: "We expect Phase 1 to deliver 0.6–1.0 information ratio (vs the paper's 1.0–1.6 globally), reflecting the 3-year window, US-only geography, missing earnings data, and non-neutralized features."

2. **Weak justification for Phase 1 factor selection** — The draft lists the 12 factors but does not explain *why* these 12 were chosen over others. Did you select them because they are easiest to compute? Or because they are most relevant to mean reversion? Or because they match the paper's priority? Add a sentence: "Phase 1 factors were selected to prioritize short-term reversal signals (R1W, RSI, momentum) and basic risk/value metrics; the selection favors tractability (Databento + SimFin availability) over the paper's broader library, which is a Phase 2 priority."

3. **Weekday-effect narrative without implementation** — The draft devotes an entire subsection (Results > Weekday Effects) to explaining the paper's weekday mechanism (Thursday > Monday due to data release timing) but then admits "raises NotImplementedError." This is wasted words if the diagnostic is not implemented. Either implement the check or move this to Limitations and signal that it is deferred. Do not present the paper's narrative as if Phase 1 can validate it.

4. **Turnover table structure** — The Turnover and Liquidity subsection presents the paper's turnover figures (212%, 317%, 341%) but does not set expectations for Phase 1. Add a row to the table: "Phase 1 expectation: 240–280% (higher than paper due to fewer, less-correlated factors; lower than basic R1W due to ensemble diversification)." This sets a testable prediction.

5. **Missing explicit acknowledgment of the Exec Summary's own hedging** — The Exec Summary uses multiple hedging phrases ("preliminary," "structural checks," "promise on structural checks," "proof-of-concept, not replication"). This is appropriate, but it is worth calling out in a footnote that the entire report is conditional on backtest execution. The Conclusion does this well, but the Exec Summary could be tightened. Remove "preliminary validation shows promise" and replace with "will show whether"; i.e., "This run will show whether a simplified, US-centric version of the industry research framework reproduces key structural findings."

6. **Regime identification too vague** — The draft mentions "rising rates, Fed tightening, and post-pandemic normalization, followed by 2024–2025 AI enthusiasm" but does not operationalize what you mean by "regime." If you are going to invoke regime dependence, define it: by VIX quintiles? Yield curve shape? Sector leadership? Or acknowledge: "The 2023–2026 window is dominated by Fed tightening followed by AI enthusiasm; mean-reversion signal strength in these regimes is unknown and will be observable post-backtest."

7. **ADV filtering mentioned but not justified** — The Data section notes that the $3M ADV floor and $20M scaling rule "have not been wired into the Phase 1 pipeline." This is fine, but the draft should explain *why* it's safe to skip. E.g., "All S&P 500 constituents have >$50M daily volume; the $3M floor is not binding." Currently, this gap is just listed without justification.

8. **Neutralization impact understated** — The draft says impact is "likely modest (say, ±0.1–0.2 IR points)" but does not justify this range. The paper's neutralization is explicitly designed to isolate cross-sectional signal; skipping it could increase residual collinearity substantially. Either cite evidence for the ±0.1–0.2 range or say: "The impact of skipping neutralization is difficult to predict; IR could be higher (if raw features contain useful noise) or lower (if collinearity drowns out signals). Post-backtest analysis will examine this via retraining with neutralized features."

9. **"Materially limit" is hedging without quantification** — The Exec Summary states "five critical comparability caveats that *materially* limit inference" but then assigns Phase 1 to a "0.6–1.0 IR" range in the Conclusion without showing the math. If caveats are "critical," show the delta: Paper 1.6 IR (global) → estimated Phase 1 1.0 IR (US, 3yr, 12 factors, no earnings data, raw features, survivorship) = 0.6 IR reduction. This transparency is stronger than "materially."

10. **Jegadeesh citation incomplete** — The Motivation section cites "Jegadeesh (1990)" and the paper does the same (page 2, footnote 1). However, the draft does not quote or summarize Jegadeesh beyond the paper's framing. If citing a paper you have not read, note it: "Jegadeesh (1990, as cited in industry research) established..." This is a minor point but maintains integrity.

11. **SHAP analysis section disconnected from model training** — The Feature Importance subsection describes what SHAP values mean and what the paper found, but does not explain when/how Phase 1 will compute them. Add: "Once the model is trained on the full backtest window, SHAP values will be computed using the standard TreeSHAP algorithm, aggregated across all test periods, and ranked by mean absolute value. We will compare Phase 1 ranks to the paper's R1W (rank 1) and UPDOWN1W (rank 2)."

12. **Survivorship bias quantification too broad** — "50–100bp per year" is a range, not a point estimate. For Phase 1, it matters. A 50bp annual bias over 3 years is 150bp cumulative return; a 100bp bias is 300bp. This is not "material but not dominant" if IR is expected to be 0.6–1.0 (i.e., 60–100bp annualized alpha). Rephrase: "Survivorship bias likely inflates Phase 1 alpha by 50–100bp annually, which is a material fraction of the expected signal. This bias will be partially mitigated in Phase 2 via point-in-time universe membership."

## Nits

1. **Wordiness in Motivation section** — The second paragraph of Motivation is 4 sentences and could be compressed. "The paper's core claim is that..." and "The headline evidence for this claim is striking:" could be merged. Minor editorial polish.

2. **Table formatting inconsistency** — The "Headline Performance Metrics" table uses "Not yet produced" for all Phase 1 cells. Consider a consistent placeholder (e.g., "—" or "TBD") to reduce visual clutter.

3. **Footnote opportunity missed** — The draft mentions "SHAP values lead the field of ML interpretability" (paraphrased from the paper, page 6). This statement (from the paper) is not a footnote; it would be cleaner as an inline cite: "Shapley values (SHAP, cited in industry research as leading ML interpretability methods)..."

4. **Conclusion tone shifts** — The Conclusion shifts from technical detail ("78-week training, 26-week validation") to forward-looking recommendation ("Prioritize (a) wiring neutralization"). The structure is sound, but the subsection **"Recommendation for Phase 2"** feels like it belongs in a separate **"Next Steps"** section rather than Conclusion. Consider reorganizing: Conclusion (summary of what was / was not validated) + Next Steps (Phase 2 priorities).

5. **Parenthetical asides in Methodology** — Phrases like "(placeholder; currently returns R1W)" and "(NOT wired; main.py does not pass market_close)" appear in data tables and inline. Cleaner to move these to a separate "Implementation Status" table referenced in a footnote, or a dedicated subsection. Currently, the reader has to parse parentheses to know what works vs. what doesn't.

6. **Expected value for Phase 1 IR not in Objective** — The Objective section lists "eight testable claims" but does not set a numerical hypothesis for IR or return. The Conclusion mentions "0.6–1.0 IR" as an estimate, but this should appear in Objective as a stated null hypothesis. E.g., "Null hypothesis: Phase 1 IR ≥ 0.6 (vs. paper's global 1.6)."
