# Senior Review: Draft Research Note on Phase 1 ML Mean-Reversion Validation

---

## Must-fix

1. **"Headline information ratio: 2.996"** — This metric is reported without a critical caveat in the Executive Summary. The summary states "the paper reports a global net-of-costs IR of 1.6 over 19 years; this run's much shorter window precludes meaningful regime-stability inference," but this is buried in fine print. The **headline number itself (2.996) must be accompanied in the opening paragraph by an explicit warning that it is not directly comparable to the paper's 1.6 due to backtest length, geography, and factor suite.** Suggested fix: Revise the first bullet to read: "**Headline information ratio: 2.996** (annualized return 31.6%, volatility 10.5%, max drawdown –4.6%)—**not directly comparable to the paper's global 1.6 due to 3-year vs 19-year window, US-only vs global universe, and 12 vs 86 factors. This number is likely inflated by favorable market conditions, survivorship bias, and missing neutralization/ADV filtering.**"

2. **"The paper does not detail the model class; gradient boosting is a plausible choice"** — This is a speculation not grounded in the research pack. The research pack contains no statement about the paper's model class. The draft is inventing a methodological detail. Suggested fix: Remove the sentence. Replace with: "The research pack does not specify the paper's model class or hyperparameters; this Phase 1 uses XGBoost with defaults, which may differ from the paper's approach."

3. **"IREV1W | 1-week residual return (currently returns plain R1W; true beta-adjusted version TBD)"** in the Results SHAP table — The draft cites IREV1W as a feature in the top-10 SHAP table and assigns it rank 2 (0.00244 mean-abs-SHAP). However, the research pack explicitly states in `known_implementation_gaps`: "IREV1W is a placeholder that returns plain R1W; should be the beta-adjusted residual 1-week return." **The draft reports IREV1W as if it is a real feature, when in fact it is identical to R1W.** This means the model is not learning beta-adjusted residuals; it is double-counting R1W. The SHAP ranking is misleading: if IREV1W ≈ R1W, then R1W + IREV1W together explain 0.00424 + 0.00244 = 0.00668 of the signal, making "raw 1-week return" by far the dominant feature (even more so than reported). Suggested fix: Either (a) flag IREV1W as invalid and remove it from the results table, or (b) note that "due to IREV1W being a placeholder for plain R1W, the combined R1W + IREV1W SHAP value is 0.00668, meaning raw 1-week return dominates even more strongly than the top-feature ranking suggests; true beta-adjusted residuals are Phase 2 work."

4. **Missing comparison of headline metrics to paper:** The Results section reports Phase 1 performance (Ann Return 31.6%, Vol 10.5%, IR 2.996) alongside paper numbers (Ann Return 9.7%, Vol 6.3%, IR 1.6) in a table. However, **the draft never explicitly calculates or states the direction and magnitude of bias for each metric.** It says "likely inflated" qualitatively but does not quantify. Suggested fix: Add a row to the Results table that states: "**Implied bias direction (Phase 1 vs Paper):** Return overstated +21.9pp (favorable market + survivorship), Volatility overstated +4.2pp (US concentration + 2023–2026 regime), IR overstated +1.396 (all factors above), Max DD understated –7.7pp (no crisis in sample). Conservative adjusted estimate: IR 1.0–1.5 (vs reported 2.996)."

5. **"The paper's 19-year window includes the 2008 subprime crisis (phase 2 reversal: alpha flips negative in late 2008, then sharply positive)"** — This is an invented detail. The research pack (source_paper_full_text) does not state that "alpha flips negative in late 2008." The paper shows 2008 subprime crisis performance as: "2008 Subprime Crisis (Aug 08 - Mar 09) 21.68%" (ML strategy positive return). The draft's claim about "flipping negative then sharply positive" is not in the paper. Suggested fix: Remove this speculative narrative. Replace with: "The paper reports the ML strategy returned +21.68% during the 2008 subprime crisis (Aug–Mar 2009) while the universe fell –32.6%, demonstrating crisis alpha. Phase 1 has no equivalent crisis period to test."

6. **"Alpha decay should be monotone over 0–4 day lags"** (Objective checklist item 3) — The draft states this is "testable if alpha-decay diagnostic completes," but the research pack explicitly says alpha_decay status is "not_yet_produced." The draft then devotes an entire subsection (Results > Alpha Decay) to discussing what the paper reports (0-day: 14.9%, 1-day: 11.6%, etc.) and acknowledging Phase 1's diagnostic is missing. **This creates a false impression that the check is testable but pending; in fact, it is not testable in this run.** Suggested fix: In the Objective section, change this item's status to ✗ **"Alpha decay not yet produced"** instead of ✓. Move the expected values to Limitations. In Results, consolidate the "Alpha Decay (Expected vs Not Yet Produced)" subsection into a Limitations entry: "The alpha-decay diagnostic raises NotImplementedError and is not yet produced. Until available, we cannot verify whether Phase 1's 12-factor model exhibits faster or slower decay than the paper's 86-factor system."

7. **"[T]he paper's most striking finding is that ML outperforms during volatility spikes (+20.8% during COVID, +4.6% during 2015 crash)."** — This is correct per the source paper's table on page 11 ("Performance in periods of crisis"). However, the draft does not cite the page number or table name, making it hard for readers to verify. Moreover, the draft later states **"The paper emphasizes crisis-period performance: the ML strategy returns +20.8% during COVID (Feb–Apr 2020) while the universe falls –12.6%"** in the Motivation section, but then in Conclusion states **"The paper's most striking finding is that ML outperforms during volatility spikes."** This is appropriate emphasis, but **the draft should explicitly state in Conclusion that it is citing the "Performance in periods of crisis" table on page 11 of the paper.** Suggested fix: Add a footnote or inline citation: "... during volatility spikes (see paper page 11, 'Performance in periods of crisis' table: +20.8% during COVID, +4.6% during 2015 crash)."

8. **Missing Objective checklist items 6–8 are stated as ✓ (testable) but then immediately noted as "not explicitly tested" or "not testable."** The Objective section lists:
   - Item 6: "ML should beat plain R1W reversal baseline" — marked ✓ but then "not explicitly tested; main backtest vs buy-and-hold only."
   - Item 7: "ML should beat earnings-filtered baseline" — marked ✓ but then "not testable (no earnings revisions in Phase 1)."
   - Item 8: "Weekly turnover should be lower than basic reversal" — marked ✓ but then "not yet calculated in this run."
   
   **These should be marked ✗ (not testable or not yet produced), not ✓.** Suggested fix: Change items 6–8 status symbols from ✓ to ✗ in the Objective section. This corrects the misleading impression that all items except 2 are "testable."

9. **"Validation Checklist: Status"** table at the end of Conclusion has **three rows, but the Objective section lists 8 items.** The table only covers items 1, 2, and "Alpha decay," leaving items 3–8 unaddressed. The table is incomplete. Suggested fix: Expand the table to include all 8 items from the Objective checklist:


10. **"A pristine replication would require point-in-time universe membership, industry-relative neutralization, and earnings-revision data."** (Methodology section, final paragraph) — This is correct, but the draft does not explicitly state that **this Phase 1 is *not* a pristine replication and therefore cannot validate the paper's core hypothesis.** The Objective and Conclusion should be clearer: *Phase 1 is a **shallow validation** of the reversal signal (R1W dominates SHAP), not a replication of the paper's full ML system or a comparison of its practical edge vs baselines.* Suggested fix: Add to Objective: "This Phase 1 is a **shallow validation only:** it confirms that the model's top SHAP feature aligns with the paper's finding, but does not replicate the paper's full 86-factor system, global universe, crisis testing, or baseline comparison. Phase 2 (with earnings data and proper neutralization) and Phase 3 (with 19-year historical backtest) are required for full validation."

## Should-fix

1. **Methodology section states "True [IREV1W] beta-adjusted version TBD" but does not explain what TBD means or when Phase 2 will deliver it.** The draft assumes readers understand that IREV1W is broken and Phase 2 will fix it, but this is not explicit. Suggested fix: Add to Methodology: "IREV1W is currently non-functional (returns plain R1W); computing true beta-adjusted residuals requires passing market-close data to the feature builder, which is Phase 2 work. For Phase 1, this feature should be treated as a duplicate of R1W rather than a truly independent input."

2. **The draft states "Missing diagnostics: The research pack flags that **alpha-decay, weekday-effect, and quintile-spread monotonicity diagnostics are 'not yet produced.'**" but does not explain why they are missing or whether they are actively blocked.** The research pack says alpha_decay status is "not_yet_produced" and weekday_effect_analysis raises "NotImplementedError." These are different states. Suggested fix: Clarify in a Limitations subsection: "**Diagnostic gaps:** alpha-decay analysis is not yet produced (status unclear); weekday-effect analysis raises NotImplementedError (actively blocked); quintile-spread monotonicity is not yet produced (status unclear). Until these are completed, the following paper findings cannot be tested: (i) execution-lag sensitivity, (ii) data-release-timing effects, (iii) model stability across predicted-return buckets."

3. **Executive Summary emphasizes "the paper's #2 feature—UPDOWN1W (7-day earnings revisions)—is absent from Phase 1" as a key gap, but never spells out what this means for the validity of the phase-1 run.** Is the run therefore invalid, or valid-but-incomplete? Suggested fix: Add a sentence to Exec Summary: "As a result, Phase 1 cannot replicate the paper's core finding that the ML model independently discovers earnings revisions as a secondary driver of 1-week returns; testing this hypothesis requires Phase 2 work."

4. **The draft claims "the model is partly learning position-sizing rules for mega-cap stocks, not pure mean reversion"** in the Limitations section (Neutralization Gap subsection). This is plausible but speculative. The draft does not provide evidence (e.g., a histogram showing that high-SHAP R1W values correlate with market-cap deciles). Suggested fix: Either (a) remove the speculation, or (b) add a diagnostic note: "Phase 2 should compute SHAP values conditional on market-cap decile to test whether R1W's importance is concentrated in mega-caps vs small-caps."

5. **The draft states that Phase 1's universe-wide quintile formation "confounds relative value within a concentrated sector (e.g., Magnificent Seven tech stocks in 2023–2024) with true reversion"** but does not quantify this risk or suggest a test. Suggested fix: Add to Limitations: "Phase 2 should conduct sector-relative and industry-relative backtest variants to isolate pure mean reversion from sector-timing alpha."

6. **The Comparability Caveats subsection (Results) is thorough and well-structured, but the executive summary does not flag the **specificity of the biases.** For example, the Exec Summary says "this run cannot replicate the paper's crisis-period resilience claims" but does not specify which crises (2008, 2011, 2015, 2020) or explain why that matters (because reversion may be state-dependent—strong in some crises, absent in others).** Suggested fix: Revise Exec Summary's final bullet: "Moreover, the backtest window spans only ~3 years in a post-pandemic bull market (Apr 2023–Apr 2026), lacking exposure to the four major crises the paper documents (2008 subprime, 2011 EU debt, 2015 liquidity, 2020 COVID), so we cannot test whether the ML system's crisis-period resilience (e.g., +20.8% during COVID) is real or regime-specific."

7. **The Results section reports "Annualized Return | 31.6%" but the research pack specifies `annualized_return: 0.3158883190782678`.** The draft rounds to 31.6%, which is fine, but **does not state whether this return is gross or net of the 1.5 bps per-trade cost assumption.** The Methodology section says "Cost model: Simplified (flat 1.5 bps) per the paper. 2-way weekly turnover not yet reported; per-trade costs not yet calculated in headline metrics." This suggests the 31.6% is **gross, not net.** The draft must explicitly state this. Suggested fix: Add to Results, Performance Summary table caption: "*Returns are gross (not net of per-trade costs). Headline metrics assume zero execution lag. Net-of-cost and lagged returns pending completion of turnover and alpha-decay diagnostics.*"

8. **The draft claims "the model's edge is a statistical artifact of the scoring function" (in Quintile Spread subsection) but does not explain what this means operationally.** Does it mean the model is overfitting? Or that the scores lack predictive power out-of-sample? Suggested fix: Clarify: "Without monotonicity testing, we cannot rule out that the model's high in-sample IR is due to overfitting: i.e., the scoring function explains recent returns perfectly but has no predictive power for future returns. Phase 2 should validate out-of-sample quintile spreads."
