# Review of Draft: Machine Learning for Weekly Mean Reversion

## Must-fix

1. **"This **exceeds the paper's global net-of-costs IR (1.6) numerically**"** — This sentence is false. The draft then correctly states the ensemble IR is 1.24, which is 0.36 units *below* 1.6, not above. The contradiction makes the executive summary misleading on first read. Fix: "This trails the paper's global net-of-costs IR (1.6 vs 1.24, a shortfall of 0.36 units)."

2. **"The 1-week reversal factor (R1W) ranks **first in SHAP importance**"** — This is factually incorrect per the draft's own Results section, which shows VOL6M ranks first (0.0182 SHAP) and R1W ranks second (0.0099 SHAP). The executive summary must acknowledge this inversion. Fix: "The 1-week reversal factor (R1W) ranks second in SHAP importance, behind volatility (VOL6M), contrary to the paper's finding that R1W is #1."

3. **Missing comparison of VOL6M finding to paper** — The draft reports VOL6M as the #1 SHAP feature across all three ensemble members, a material divergence from the paper's stated R1W #1 ranking. The paper's full text (page 6–7) explicitly shows "Shap values of the weekly ML model" with R1W as the top feature. The draft documents this disagreement in Results but does not quantify or reconcile it in the Motivation or Conclusions. The draft notes "One interpretation: the ensemble is hedging reversion bets with volatility exposure," but this is speculation unsupported by ablation or sensitivity analysis. Add a Must-fix flag: the narrative should either (a) explain why VOL6M > R1W in this data (e.g., "FMP's price data is noisier, inflating estimated volatility") or (b) acknowledge the gap as an unexplained deviation from the paper's finding.

4. **UPDOWN1W_RATINGS is ranked 10th in this run, not 2nd as the paper's #2 SHAP feature** — The draft clearly states "UPDOWN1W_RATINGS lags earnings revisions by ~1–2 weeks" and achieves "~0.1% of R1W's importance" (0.0004 SHAP vs 0.0099). Yet the Objective section (item 2) categorizes this as "not evaluable," and the Conclusion defers to "not testable." This is soft language for a critical failure: **the proxy is demonstrably ineffective**, and the model is missing the paper's #2 alpha driver. The draft must upgrade this to a Must-fix in the Conclusion and recommend retraining with actual EPS revision data (e.g., from SimFin or Refinitiv) or documenting the earnings-momentum gap as a replication failure. Suggested fix: "The model cannot validate the paper's #2 SHAP finding (UPDOWN1W = 7-day EPS revisions), as FMP analyst-rating revisions rank 10th and contribute <0.1% of R1W's signal strength. This is a critical gap. Replication would require historical consensus EPS-estimate data."

5. **"the run validates the *concept* (R1W is important, weekday effect exists, alpha decays with lag) but does not replicate the paper's specific 2006–2025 evidence."** — This conclusion is internally contradicted by the executive summary ("This run tests whether that claim holds") and by the Objective section, which lists seven testable items. The draft must be explicit upfront: does this run validate or invalidate the paper's findings? The current narrative is ambiguous. Suggested fix: rewrite the Conclusion to state: "This run confirms mean reversion and weekday timing are real and actionable, but does not replicate the paper's quantitative evidence (IR 1.24 vs 1.6) due to survivorship bias, missing earnings revisions, and a recent window."

6. **Weekday effect results are not directly comparable to the paper** — The draft reports Friday returns of 35.4% annualized vs the paper's ~24% (inferred from the paper's statement that Thursday has 28% of releases and is "the most common release date"). The draft notes "The paper does not report Friday explicitly, only Thursday and Monday" but then claims "The weekday effect is qualitatively robust." This is misleading: if Friday > Thursday in this run, it contradicts the paper's implicit claim that Thursday is the peak. The paper states "strategies...rebalanced using Thursday's data perform significantly better than those rebalanced using Monday's data" (page 8). This run's Friday 35.4% > Thursday 30.6% is a meaningful reordering. Flag this as a deviation and explain it (e.g., "end-of-week repricing") rather than burying it as a minor note. Add: "This suggests the paper's Thursday peak may be specific to the 2006–2025 sample or to its larger factor set; the 13-factor model exhibits stronger late-Friday reversion."

7. **Missing reconciliation of alpha-decay steepness** — The draft reports decay of 13.8% → 4.9% over 4 days (65% loss) vs the paper's 14.9% → 6.8% (53% loss). The draft attributes this to "narrower universe (smaller mean reversion effect)," but provides no evidence. If the US portion of the paper had comparable steepness to the 3-region universe here, this would be evidence that the factor set (not geography) is the limiting factor. The paper's US-only performance is not disclosed here. Add a sensitivity analysis: "To isolate the factor-set effect, a follow-up run would train the 13-factor model on the paper's full 86-factor universe using FMP data, holding geography constant."

8. **"Turnover is nearly identical across the four (56.9%–59.8%), so the ensemble's outperformance is not driven by reduced rebalancing."** — The draft then later states "Lower turnover *directly* improves net-of-cost returns: fewer round-trip costs (1.5 bps × 2). At 1.5 bps/side, 212% turnover costs ~64 bps/week (3.3% annualized); this run's 59.8% costs ~18 bps/week (0.9% annualized), a 2.4% advantage." This is a **direct contradiction**. If ensemble turnover (59.8%) is similar to member turnover (56.9–58.6%), then the ensemble's IR improvement (+0.12 units) is not from lower costs; conversely, if the 59.8% turnover is lower than traditional reversal (317% or 212%), then lower costs *should* explain the outperformance relative to the paper. The draft conflates two comparisons: (1) ensemble vs members (turnover ~ equal) and (2) ensemble vs paper (turnover much lower). Rewrite to clarify: "Ensemble vs members: turnover is ~57–60%, so ensemble outperformance is driven by prediction quality, not reduced rebalancing. Ensemble vs paper: turnover is 59.8% vs paper's 212% (ML) / 317% (basic), implying 2.4% less cost drag—but the gross alpha is also lower, so the cost benefit does not fully explain the IR gap."

9. **Missing explicit statement of factor inventory validation** — The draft does not confirm that all 13 factors in the run are present in the `factor_inventory` and `phase1_factor_inventory` fields of the research pack. Spot-check: VOL6M ✓, R1W ✓, UPDOWN1W_RATINGS ✓, etc. The pack lists these 13 factors verbatim. However, the draft should explicitly state in Methodology: "All 13 factors are sourced from the run's factor_inventory and validated against the research pack; UPDOWN1W_RATINGS is a novel FMP-based approximation not in the paper's original library." This prevents later confusion about whether factors are invented.

10. **"the model naturally rediscovers mean reversion and earnings momentum as the dominant predictors"** — This is quoted from the paper (page 2) but is NOT validated by the run. The run does rediscover mean reversion (R1W is top-3 SHAP), but **earnings momentum is ranked 10th** (UPDOWN1W_RATINGS = 0.0004 SHAP). The draft must not quote the paper as if it were validated here. Fix: "The paper claims the model 'rediscovers mean reversion and earnings momentum as the dominant predictors' (page 2). This run confirms mean reversion but cannot validate earnings momentum due to missing EPS-revision data."

11. **Quintile formation: region-only or region × sector?** — The Methodology section states "Form quintiles within region (not specified if × sector; likely global region)." This is acknowledged as ambiguous, but the downstream impact is material (as the draft notes: "If quintiles are universe-global, it will overweight sector bets"). The portfolio construction algorithm must specify this unambiguously, and the results must be flagged if sector neutrality is not enforced. Suggested fix: add a line to Portfolio Construction: "Quintiles are formed within each region globally (not sector-neutral). This may induce sector tilts and bias results toward momentum-heavy and large-cap sectors in the sample period. A sector-neutral variant is recommended for validation."

12. **Warm-up period degradation not quantified** — The draft notes "Early weeks (2008-2008) have reduced signal quality due to missing-data zero-fill" but does not report results for the warm-up vs post-warm-up period. If the 2008–2009 IR is materially lower than 2010–2026, this would explain much of the gap to the paper's 1.6 IR (the paper's full window includes the high-reversion crisis period at full signal quality). Add: "Sensitivity analysis: IR from 2010 onward (post-warm-up) is [report if available]; this would isolate the impact of rolling-factor initialization."

## Should-fix

1. **Executive summary claims "annexes the core mean-reversion and weekday-timing intuitions but cannot claim to replicate"** — This phrasing ("annexes") is non-standard and may be a typo for "anchors" or "advances." If intentional, define it. Otherwise fix to "validates" or "confirms."

2. **Paper-reference metadata mismatch** — The research pack's `paper_reference.top_shap_features` lists ["R1W (1-week return) — most important", "UPDOWN1W (7-day EPS upward minus downward revisions) — second most important"]. The draft correctly quotes this. However, the pack's `paper_reference.alpha_decay_global` is labeled as [0: 0.149, 1: 0.116, ...], implying 0-day lag is 14.9% (matching the paper's page 7 chart). The draft cites these correctly but should note that the paper's chart is unlabeled for whether 0-day means "trade at close on day signal is computed" or "trade at open next day." Assuming 0-day = Wednesday close (compute signal, trade same day), this run's 0-day 13.8% would assume the same assumption. Add a note: "Alpha-decay lags are defined consistently with the paper: 0-day = same-day execution (Wednesday close compute → trade), 1-day = next-day execution, etc."

3. **Per-member SHAP disagreement flagged but not investigated** — The draft notes "All three members agree VOL6M is #1, but disagree on the 2–3 ranking." It speculates that RandomForest "suggests momentum tail-drag in tree splits" but does not investigate whether this is overfitting, data imbalance, or genuine signal difference. A brief ablation (e.g., "Removing R12M1M and retraining RF shows [result]") would clarify. Add: "This disagreement warrants further investigation; it may indicate that RandomForest is overfitting to mid-horizon momentum in the 10-year training window."

4. **Cost-model simplification section is weak** — The draft lists borrow costs (0.5–2% annualized), market impact (2–5 bps), and concludes "The true cost is likely 2.0–2.5 bps one-way, or 4–5 bps round-trip. This would reduce net returns by 0.2–0.4% annualized." But 0.2–0.4% is ~0.02–0.04% of the 10% return, a rounding error. The arithmetic here is wrong: 60% 2-way turnover × 5 bps = 300 bps / 52 weeks = 5.8 bps/week = 0.3% annualized drag, not 0.2–0.4%. Fix the math and clarify: "If true costs are 2.5 bps one-way (vs assumed 1.5 bps), the additional drag is ~0.2% annualized on 60% turnover, reducing the 10.0% return to 9.8% — a small but material gap."

5. **Missing disclosure of whether the paper's "80 common equity factors" includes the weekly-specific factors or not** — The paper says "We keep the 80 common equity factors" then adds "However, for the weekly model, we have added six factors with shorter timeframes." This implies the weekly model has 86 total factors (80 + 6). The draft cites "80+ equity factors" correctly but then says "This run's 13-factor set is a simplified subset." The contrast would be clearer if stated as "Paper's weekly model: 86 factors (80 baseline + 6 weekly-specific). This run: 13 factors (5 reversals, 2 momentum, 2 risk, 3 value, 1 profitability, [recalculate]), a ~7:1 compression."

6. **Ensemble rank-mean aggregation is stated as "a reasonable modern approach but is not directly validated against the paper."** — The paper may use an ensemble; it is not disclosed. However, the claim that "rank-mean" is "not validated" is overstated: the ensemble outperforms all three members on IR (1.24 vs 1.11, 1.01, 1.12), which is evidence of its validity on this data. Add: "Rank-mean aggregation improves IR by +0.12 over the best single member (RandomForest 1.12 → Ensemble 1.24), consistent with diversity-driven ensemble benefits. The paper does not disclose its aggregation method, so direct validation is not possible."

7. **"The paper's 0d → 4d curve is 14.9% → 6.8% (53% loss). This run is 13.8% → 4.9% (65% loss), steeper."** — The percentages here are confusing. "53% loss" means (6.8 - 14.9) / 14.9 = -54.3%, not 53%. And "65% loss" is (4.9 - 13.8) / 13.8 = -64.5%, not 65%. The difference in decay *rate* (65% vs 53%) is material and well-stated, but the absolute percentages are off by ~1%. This is a nit, but precision matters: fix to "(53.4% loss)" or simply state "the decay is ~10 percentage points steeper."

8. **Quintile returns monotonicty is not reported** — The Objective lists (item 5) "Per-quintile returns are monotonic (Q1 worst, Q5 best)." The draft states "(not reported; assumed valid from factor importance)" under the Validation Checklist Summary. This is not rigorous. Monotonicty is testable and should be reported: add a line to Results, "Quintile performance (net of costs, annualized return): Q1 [X]%, Q2 [Y]%, ..., Q5 [Z]%, with monotonic ordering confirmed / violated." If the pack has weekly long/short quintile returns, extract them.

9. **The phrase "honest distinct name"** (in the factor inventory, referring to UPDOWN1W_RATINGS) is colloquial and should be removed or rephrased to "to distinguish from the paper's EPS-revision-based UPDOWN1W."
