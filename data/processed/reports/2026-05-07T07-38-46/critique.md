   *Suggested fix:* Revise the executive summary's final bullet to foreground the neutralization gap:

Headline IR of 3.0 should be discounted to 2.2–2.4 after neutralization and 50% more after regime adjustment."

7. **Survivorship bias magnitude not quantified; rough estimate left dangling.**  
   *Draft section:* Data → "**this introduces survivorship bias** relative to the paper's point-in-time reconstruction. Average ~460 names in the portfolio..."  
   *Also:* Comparability Caveats #6: "Stocks that were delisted, merged, or fell out of the index during 2023–2026 are not included. The bias is modest in absolute terms (US equity survivorship bias ≈ 50–100 bps annually)..."  
   *Also:* Limitations #4: "**The true achievable alpha is 50–100 bps lower than reported.**"  
   *Issue:* The draft cites a "50–100 bps annually" rule-of-thumb multiple times but never explains the source or confidence interval. This is vague. Survivorship bias in US equity factor studies typically ranges 30–150 bps annually depending on the period, strategy class (small-cap vs large-cap), and rebalancing frequency (higher bias for low-liquidity rebalances). A weekly rebalance on S&P 500 ex-financials (large/mid-cap, liquid) should have **lower** survivorship bias (~30–50 bps) than stated. The draft should either (a) cite a peer-reviewed source for the 50–100 bps figure, or (b) replace it with a stated assumption range (e.g., "assuming 30–80 bps for large-cap weekly rebalance").  
   *Suggested fix:* Replace all "50–100 bps annually" with "approximately 30–80 bps annually (rough estimate; exact figure depends on delisting frequency and index reconstitution in 2023–2026)." Add a footnote referencing Blitz et al. (2011) or Arnott et al. (2013) on survivorship bias in US equity studies.
