# Critique of Analyst Draft: "Machine Learning for Short-Term Equity Reversion"

## Must-fix

1. **SHAP ranking claim contradicts paper without acknowledgment** — The draft states: "R1W (1-week return) ranks #2, confirming the paper's thesis that short-term reversals are material signals, even if VOL6M (volatility control) ranks higher." The paper's text explicitly states: "The most important feature is the past 1-week return" and "Below is a chart showing the SHAP values for the 10 most important features of our ML model" with R1W labeled as the top feature. The draft reports VOL6M as #1 (0.0182) and R1W as #2 (0.0099). This is a **factual inversion of the paper's core claim**. The draft acknowledges it occurs but frames it as acceptable ("R1W #2 still confirms mean reversion"). It does not adequately explain why the paper's #1 becomes this run's #1, nor does it investigate whether this is a critical failure of the replication. Must be reframed: either (a) explain the mechanism causing VOL6M dominance in this run vs. R1W in the paper (is it the 13-factor set? the training window? multi-region?), or (b) flag this as a **failed validation of Objective #1**.

2. **Weekday effect magnitudes are unexplained 10× divergence** — The draft reports Monday 22.1%, Thursday 30.6%, vs. the paper's Monday 2.2%, Thursday 3.1%. The draft acknowledges this ("Reported weekday returns in this run are ~10× higher than the paper's... likely a data artifact") but provides **no root-cause investigation or correction**. It then immediately uses these inflated figures to validate the weekday ranking. This violates basic due diligence: if you believe the figures are erroneous, you cannot cite them as evidence. The section states: "The **ranking** (Friday > Thursday > ... > Monday) is informative and consistent with the paper; the **absolute values** are unreliable." But the validation checklist marks "Thursday > Monday weekday effect ✅ Pass" with the implausible 30.6% vs 22.1% numbers. This is internally contradictory and misleading. **Must clarify**: either investigate the root cause (sampling, cost treatment, survivorship), recompute correctly, or explicitly exclude weekday validation from the checklist pending investigation.

3. **Turnover unit ambiguity is unresolved and corrupts the comparison** — The draft states: "The research pack reports `avg_weekly_turnover: 0.586050551743235`, which we interpret as 0.586 or 58.6% per week." It then proposes two interpretations:
   - Interpretation A: 58.6% one-way weekly → 30.5% annualized one-way, or 61% two-way, "materially higher than the paper's ML (212% or 2.1% weekly two-way)."
   - Interpretation B: 58.6% "weekly two-way" → ~29.3% annualized two-way, "still much lower than the paper's 212%."
   
   The draft **does not resolve which is correct** and proceeds to validate against both. The research pack metadata states: `"config_train_weeks": 78, ... "avg_weekly_turnover": 0.586050551743235`—no units. Without clarification from the backtest code, the draft should **not cite turnover as a validation point**. Instead, it should state: "Turnover unit is ambiguous (raw fraction vs. percentage, one-way vs. two-way); cannot compare to paper until clarified." The validation checklist marks "Turnover < basic reversal ✅ Tentative" but this is not "tentative"—it is **unvalidatable**.

4. **Missing required section: Baseline Strategy Comparison** — The Objective section sets Objective #6: "Does the ML strategy achieve positive net-of-cost returns with reasonable Sharpe / IR?" and notes "Paper: Global net-of-cost IR = 1.6; US net-of-cost IR = 1.0. This run: IR = 1.11." However, the paper reports performance for **three strategies: (i) ML, (ii) Basic Price Reversals, (iii) Earnings-Filtered Price Reversals**. The draft provides no baseline comparison. The paper's key claims include: "It achieves better absolute performance while reducing risk" and "The model demonstrates more consistent returns and lower seasonality, along with significantly reduced turnover, leading to lower execution costs and strong cost-adjusted performance." Without reporting baseline strategy performance in this run, the draft **cannot validate the relative benefit of ML**. The validation checklist item "ML beats earnings-filtered reversal ❌ Not testable" is correct but represents a **critical gap** that should have been flagged in the Objective or anticipated in the Data section (e.g., "baseline strategies will be constructed from long/short quintiles of R1W and UPDOWN1W_RATINGS"). This is a missing **required deliverable**.

5. **Paper's "second most important feature" claim is untestable but not flagged clearly** — The draft correctly notes that "UPDOWN1W_RATINGS (analyst rating revisions) ranks 10th. The true UPDOWN1W (EPS revisions) is absent." However, it does not make explicit what the paper actually says. From `source_paper_full_text` page 7: "The model also highlights the importance of earnings-related metrics, particularly the 7-day EPS factor, which ranks as the second-most important feature." This is a **core claim of the paper**. The draft's Objective #2 asks: "Does UPDOWN1W (earnings revisions) emerge as the second-most-important feature?" and answers: "Paper answer: Yes... In this run, UPDOWN1W is unavailable (replaced by analyst-rating proxies); we note this gap explicitly." But this is buried in the Objective section and never revisited in Results. The draft should have a dedicated subsection in Results stating: **"Critical Gap: UPDOWN1W (EPS Revisions) Cannot Be Tested."** Explaining that EPS-revision factors require IBES/Refinitiv historical data (not available from FMP), and that this run's UPDOWN1W_RATINGS (ranking 10th) cannot substitute because it measures analyst sentiment (broad rating changes) vs. earnings consensus changes (granular estimate revisions). **The paper's second-most-important finding is untestable in this implementation.** This must be elevated to a "Cannot Validate" in the checklist, not buried.

6. **Paper's "remarkable" claim about model rediscovery is overstated without evidence** — The Motivation section asserts: "The result is striking: the model **organically identifies mean reversion factors as its highest-importance signals** (R1W as #1 SHAP, UPDOWN1W as #2), effectively rediscovering in hours what took the quant desk weeks to hypothesize." However, in Results, this run shows **R1W as #2, not #1**, and **UPDOWN1W_RATINGS as #10, not #2**. The draft later acknowledges this but doesn't walk back the Motivation framing. The Motivation section is describing the paper's result, not this run's, so this is defensible—but the narrative flow is misleading: a reader finishing Motivation expects Results to confirm "R1W #1, UPDOWN1W #2" and instead finds the opposite. **Reframe Motivation** to clearly attribute the "organic discovery" claim to the paper alone, and preview in Objective that this run will test whether the same holds in a multi-region 13-factor context (spoiler: it does not for VOL6M dominance).

7. **"Provisional hypothesis testing" framing is too permissive** — The Executive Summary concludes: "These gaps prevent a clean one-to-one replication but allow provisional hypothesis testing." This is used as cover for multiple unresolved issues (survivorship bias, missing factors, SHAP inversion). However, the draft then **proceeds to validate objectives against this "provisional" framework** without flagging which validations are truly provisional vs. failed. The validation checklist should distinguish:
   - ✅ **Passed**: Alpha decay monotonic (0d–4d) [testable with available factors].
   - ❌ **Failed**: R1W as #1 SHAP [testable; result contradicts paper].
   - ⚠️ **Not Testable**: UPDOWN1W as #2 SHAP [missing factor].
   - ❓ **Unreliable Data**: Weekday effect magnitudes [10× divergence; source unclear].
   - ❌ **Not Testable (By Design)**: Baseline strategy comparison [no baseline constructed].
   
   The current checklist conflates these categories. **Redo the checklist** with explicit status codes.

8. **Known gap: Quintile formation across full universe, not peer-group relative** — The research pack flags: "Quintiles formed across the full universe; paper forms within region × GICS industry peer groups. Phase 2 work." The draft mentions this in Limitations (#5) but does not quantify the bias. The paper's peer-group-relative approach is designed to reduce sector/size exposure and tighten alpha. Full-universe quintiles will naturally have higher sector dispersion and may inflate returns if sector tilts are unintended factor exposures. The draft estimates "likely upward bias on returns if sector/size are untreated factor exposures" but does not investigate whether this explains the VOL6M dominance (a global vol factor might dominate in full-universe ranking but not in sector-relative ranking). **Add to Results**: an analysis of sector and size exposures in the long/short portfolio, or a note that this cannot be computed from the available research pack data.

9. **Turnover interpretation uses contradictory logic** — The draft states: "If the research pack's 58.6% is one-way weekly, the annualized equivalent is 30.5% one-way or 61% two-way, **significantly higher than the paper's ML (212% or 2.1% weekly two-way)**." But 212% annualized two-way = 4.1% per week one-way, and 61% annualized two-way = 1.17% per week one-way. So this run's turnover (61% annualized two-way) is **lower than the paper's ML (212%)**, not higher. The draft then says: "This run's turnover is in between, suggesting a 'medium' complexity model relative to the paper's extremes." This contradicts the prior sentence. **Fix the math and interpretation**: 61% annualized two-way is indeed lower than 212%, supporting the claim that "ML reduces turnover." But the draft's wording creates confusion.

10. **"Paper's earnings-revision factors (UPDOWN1W as EPS revisions, SUE1W/SUE3/SUE6)" —reference to paper accuracy** — The draft cites the paper as identifying these three factors as critical. From `source_paper_full_text`, page 13 (Addendum I), the full factor library is listed, including UPDOWN1W, SUE1W, SUE3, SUE6. But on page 7 (SHAP discussion), the paper only emphasizes R1W and UPDOWN1W (7-day EPS revisions) as the top two. The draft should clarify: "The paper's top two SHAP features are R1W (#1) and UPDOWN1W (#2). This run lacks UPDOWN1W (EPS revisions) and all three SUE variants (SUE1W, SUE3, SUE6)." SUE factors are in the paper's library but not the primary SHAP focus; conflating them with UPDOWN1W risks overstating the impact of their absence.

## Should-fix

1. **Comparability Caveats section is buried in Results; should be elevated to Methodology or placed before Results** — The draft places a subsection titled "Comparability Caveats" at the end of the Results section. This is correct placement in terms of narrative flow, but the Executive Summary should **lead** with the most material caveat. The current Executive Summary notes: "Comparability caveat: The run uses 13 factors, the paper used 86..." but this is the **third-to-last sentence**. Readers expecting 1:1 replication will skim the Executive Summary, see headline metrics (9.1% return, 1.11 IR), and assume the paper is replicated. Only later will they discover the factor count mismatch. **Reorder**: Move the first 1–2 sentences of Comparability Caveats into the Executive Summary's first paragraph, so any reader immediately understands this is a **limited replication in a different regime and universe composition**.

2. **Data section does not state the observation count or confirm sufficient data availability for multi-region backtest** — The draft says: "The test window is **2006-01-01 through 2026-04-11, a span of 958 weeks**." But it does not report: (i) how many stocks per region per week, (ii) what fraction of the S&P 500 is included (average over the window), (iii) whether UK and Canada universes are similar in size. The research pack shows `"num_observations": 755862` (total predictions across all stocks/dates), but this is not contextualized. The draft should state: "Over the 958-week window, the US universe averaged X stocks, UK Y stocks, Canada Z stocks. The 13-factor set was complete for >95% of stocks, with <1% requiring zero-imputation beyond the 10-factor threshold." Without this, readers cannot assess whether the backtest universe is sufficiently liquid and deep.

3. **Model Class section acknowledges uncertainty but does not flag it as a limitation** — The Methodology section states: "The research pack does not specify the exact model class (random forest, XGBoost, LightGBoost, or other)... This run's implementation is inferred to follow a similar rolling-window gradient-boosted tree framework, though explicit confirmation is absent." This is buried in Methodology. It should be flagged in Limitations as item #7 or promoted: **"Model Class and Hyperparameters Unknown: Without documentation, results cannot be precisely reproduced or bench-marked against the paper's tuning choices."** This is a critical gap for transparency.

4. **Paper's claim about model discovering "weeks of research" is not contextualized** — The Motivation section quotes: "In just a few hours, the machine was able to arrive at the same conclusion that had taken us weeks of research to reach – very impressive!" The draft uses this to frame the conceptual appeal but does not note the paper's broader context: the industry research team already had strong priors about mean reversion (from prior work cited: "A reversals refresher", "Price reversals – academic curiosity or profitable investment strategy?"). The ML model was not trained blind; it was applied to a problem space where the research team suspected the answer. This is **not a criticism** but an important framing: the paper is demonstrating that ML can **confirm and enhance** hypotheses, not that it discovers surprising new patterns. The draft should note: "The paper frames this as an efficient validation of existing research hypotheses, not discovery of hidden signals."

5. **BETA6M as "proxy for SPX" using SPY is a material simplification not acknowledged** — The draft lists BETA6M as "6-month rolling beta vs SPY (proxy for SPX; main.py fetches SPY close via fetch_market_close and passes it to build_feature_matrix)." The research pack notes this. However, the draft does not discuss the implications: SPY is a US-focused ETF with slightly different dividend treatment and constituents vs. the actual S&P 500 index. For UK and Canada stocks, using SPY beta is a **region mismatch**—it measures co-movement with the US market, not the local market. The draft should acknowledge: "BETA6M uses SPY (US market) as the reference for all regions; for UK and Canada stocks, this measures USD-exposure beta, not local-market beta. This is a known gap flagged in the research pack for Phase 2 correction." This is a **data fidelity issue**, not merely a simplification.
