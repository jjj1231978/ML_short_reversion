# Checksheet: Analyst Draft vs. Research Pack

---

## Must-fix

1. **"VOL6M is the top SHAP feature" — Missing paper benchmark for SHAP ranking.**
   - The draft reports VOL6M as rank 1 (0.0182 mean abs SHAP) but does not quote or cite the paper's SHAP ranking. The paper states explicitly (page 6, "Shap values of the weekly ML model"): *"The most important feature is the past 1-week return"* and *"The model also highlights the importance of earnings-related metrics, particularly the 7-day EPS factor, which ranks as the second-most important feature."* The paper's actual ranking is: **R1W #1, UPDOWN1W #2**. The draft correctly notes this mismatch in the SHAP table but frames it as a finding rather than a failure. Suggested fix: Rewrite the SHAP section to explicitly state that the paper predicts R1W #1 and UPDOWN1W #2, and that this run achieves VOL6M #1, R1W #2, UPDOWN1W #10. This is a **failure to replicate**, not a partial success. Treat it as such.

2. **"Annualized return | 9.1% | 9.7% (Paper Global Net of Costs)" — Scope mismatch not flagged.**
   - The draft compares this run's global result (9.1%) to the paper's *global* result (9.7%). But this run is US-dominant with UK/Canada overlays, while the paper explicitly tests US + Europe + Japan. The paper also reports a US-only result (8.6%, page 10). The draft should compare apples to apples: either this run's US subset vs paper's US (8.6%), or acknowledge that the three-region mean is not the same as the paper's global developed portfolio. The caption "Paper (Global Net of Costs, 2006–2025)" masks a composition mismatch. Suggested fix: Add a sub-row for "US only (this run)" and compare to paper's US-only figure (8.6%).

3. **"Paper uses point-in-time global developed constituents (source not detailed)" — Unsubstantiated claim.**
   - The draft asserts the paper "likely avoids survivorship bias for the US but not necessarily for Europe/Japan." But the paper never states whether its universe is point-in-time or snapshot. The paper says (page 4): *"We trained the weekly model on the same global developed market large/mid-cap universe that we use for our 'live' ML index range."* It does not detail the universe construction. The draft should flag this as an **unknown** rather than speculating. Suggested fix: Revise to *"The paper does not detail whether its global universe is point-in-time or snapshot; this remains unclear."*

4. **"UPDOWN1W_RATINGS ranks tenth" vs. paper's second — Insufficient explanation.**
   - The draft acknowledges UPDOWN1W_RATINGS is a proxy (rating revisions, not EPS revisions) and suggests "proxy mismatch" and "factor redundancy." But the draft does not cite the paper's explicit definition or source. The paper states (page 6, "Most important factors"): *"the second-most important feature. This is the same factor used as an overlay in the earnings-filtered strategy."* The paper also attributes this to IBES data: *"Earnings revisions source: FMP /stable/grades (analyst rating revisions; PROXY for paper's IBES EPS revisions)"* — but wait, the research pack metadata says the earnings source is "FMP /stable/grades (analyst rating revisions; PROXY for paper's IBES EPS revisions)." This run **intentionally uses a proxy,** per the research pack. The draft should clarify: the research pack's own metadata acknowledges this is a proxy. The draft correctly flags the issue but should quote the research pack's own caveat. Suggested fix: Quote the research pack's note that UPDOWN1W_RATINGS is "*PROXY for the paper's IBES EPS revisions; same formula structure but uses analyst rating revisions instead.*"

5. **"Annualized return 13.4% (0d lag)" — Where does this baseline come from?**
   - The draft table reports "This Run (0d lag)" = 13.4%, but the headline metric in the research pack is 9.1% (the full portfolio with 1d lag). The 13.4% value appears in the research pack's `alpha_decay.by_lag["0"]` but is labeled "No Lag" and is theoretical (pre-cost or with-cost?). The paper's 0d figure (page 7, "Alpha decay") is 14.9%. The draft should clarify: is the 13.4% a cost-adjusted or un-adjusted return? The paper's comparison baseline is *net of costs*. If this run's 13.4% is pre-cost, the comparison is invalid. Suggested fix: Explicitly state whether each alpha-decay row is gross or net of transaction costs, and provide a footnote explaining the data source.

6. **"Weekday effect is pronounced: Friday signals outperform Monday by 1,130 bps annualized" — Paper's weekday effect magnitude not cited.**
   - The draft reports Friday – Monday = 35.4% – 22.1% = 13.2% annualized difference, which is *1,320 bps*, not 1,130 bps (a typo or rounding issue). More critically, the draft does NOT cite the paper's weekday-effect magnitude. The paper reports (page 8, "Global Long/Short basic price-reversal strategy based on the day we calculate the signal"): the chart shows Thursday at ~18% and Monday at ~10%, a ~800 bps spread for the basic R1W strategy. The paper's ML strategy shows similar but less pronounced weekday effects. The draft's 1,320 bps spread is **much larger** than the paper's ~800 bps. This is a major discrepancy that should be front-and-center in the Results section, not buried. Suggested fix: Quote the paper's weekday performance directly, show this run's breakdown, and highlight the magnitude difference as a key finding (possibly reflecting geographic/regime differences).

7. **"Benchmark and neutralization" section — Baselines described but not tested against.**
   - The draft says "This run evaluates the ML strategy directly; the baselines are available from the paper" and cites paper figures: basic R1W 14.9% return, earnings-filtered 12.4%. But the draft does NOT evaluate whether this run's ML strategy beats these baselines net of costs. The paper's validation checklist includes "ML beats plain R1W reversal net of costs" and "ML beats earnings-filtered R1W reversal." The draft should compute or cite the equivalent baselines in this run's data and directly compare. Currently, the draft asserts this implicitly (via lower turnover) but doesn't test it. Suggested fix: Add a new subsection "ML vs. Baselines" with a table showing this run's ML return vs this run's R1W reversal return and this run's earnings-filtered reversal return, with explicit statement of which the ML strategy beats.

8. **"Paper's richer library likely yield sharper factor competition" — Speculation, not fact.**
   - The draft says in the SHAP interpretation: *"This divergence may reflect the leaner factor set (13 vs 86): without a rich library of profitability, leverage, and earnings-quality metrics, the model may compensate by overweighting volatility as a proxy for quality."* This is a reasonable hypothesis but is not tested. The draft does not run a sensitivity analysis showing that adding more factors would shift the SHAP ranking. This is presented as fact ("likely yield") without evidence. Suggested fix: Reframe as an explicit hypothesis and note that testing it requires a 86-factor implementation (out of scope for this run).

9. **"Turnover (58.6%) is substantially lower than naive reversal (212%+)" — Missing definition of 2-way turnover.**
   - The draft reports "2-way weekly turnover" is 58.6% but does not define what "2-way" means. Does this mean sum of |long changes| + |short changes|, or something else? The paper (page 9) defines "2-way turnover per week" and shows a chart, but the specific formula is not given. The draft should define it clearly so a reader can verify the claim. Suggested fix: Add a footnote: "2-way turnover = sum of absolute position changes in long portfolio + absolute position changes in short portfolio, as a fraction of AUM."

10. **"The paper's 86-factor library likely provides sharper factor competition" — Factual basis?**
    - The draft asserts this without quoting or demonstrating it from the paper. The paper does not directly compare SHAP rankings under different factor counts. This is a reasonable inference but should be labeled as such, not stated as fact. Suggested fix: Reframe in Limitations as "Hypothesis: the paper's 86-factor library likely provides sharper factor competition, reducing spurious dominance of catch-all factors like VOL6M. Testing this requires a full 86-factor implementation."

11. **Missing validation checklist item: "Quintile monotonicity Q1→Q5"**
    - The draft's Objective section includes a checklist with 8 items, item 6 being "Does expected return increase monotonically from Q1 (worst) to Q5 (best)?" The draft then notes "This run can address items 1–4, 6–8 with available data. Item 5 (quintile monotonicity) requires per-quintile return breakdowns not present in the research pack and cannot be directly evaluated." **This is wrong.** Item 5 is "Turnover advantage" (ML vs naive R1W), item 6 is "Quintile monotonicity." The draft is off by one. Suggested fix: Renumber or clarify the checklist order. More importantly, the draft should check whether the research pack includes per-quintile return data (it does not appear to, but the draft should state this explicitly).

12. **"The paper's data vendor is IBES, but this run uses FMP analyst grades as a proxy" — Mischaracterization.**
    - The draft says: *"Earnings-revision factors (SUE1W, SUE3, SUE6): The paper uses Standardized Unexpected Earnings (EPS revisions relative to estimate volatility)... This run substitutes **UPDOWN1W_RATINGS**, a 7-day rolling tally of analyst rating upgrades minus downgrades."* But the research pack metadata explicitly states: *"Earnings revisions source: FMP /stable/grades (analyst rating revisions; PROXY for paper's IBES EPS revisions)."* The paper does NOT mention IBES by name; only the research pack infers it. The draft should cite the research pack's note, not the paper. Suggested fix: Clarify in the Data section: "The paper does not specify its earnings-revision data source in the published text; the research pack infers IBES. This run uses FMP analyst grades as a documented proxy."

## Should-fix

1. **Executive summary: "9.7% return, 6.3% volatility, and IR of 1.6" — Which paper result?**
   - The paper reports multiple results: global (9.7% / 6.3% / 1.6), US (8.6% / 8.5% / 1.0), Europe (9.8% / 8.5% / 1.2), Japan (9.7% / 8.9% / 1.1) (page 10, "Risk and return statistics"). The Exec Summary cites "the paper's global net-of-cost benchmark" without specifying which one. It should say: *"compared to the paper's global developed-market benchmark (US + Europe + Japan, net of costs): 9.7% return, 6.3% volatility, IR 1.6."* This clarifies that the comparison is to a three-region portfolio, not a two-region one.

2. **"Quintile assignment: Within each region × industry peer group (implied by industry-neutral factor design)" — Too vague.**
   - The draft claims quintiles are "implied" by industry-neutral factor design, but doesn't state it explicitly. The paper (page 5) says: *"The quintiles are calculated relative to the region and industry peer group for each stock."* This run should either (a) confirm that quintiles are formed within region × GICS industry sub-groups, or (b) state that they are formed across the full universe. The Methodology section should be crystal-clear on this. Suggested fix: Add a statement: "Quintiles are formed within each region and GICS industry sub-group, consistent with the paper's neutralization approach."

3. **"Multi-week warm-up for BETA6M (~25 weeks) and R12M1M (~252 days). Missing data zero-filled so training can begin week 1, but early-window factor quality is reduced." — Should quantify the impact.**
   - The draft notes the issue but doesn't estimate how much it biases the OOS results. Does zero-filling early factors inflate or deflate returns? The research pack notes this but doesn't estimate impact. The draft could test sensitivity by excluding the first 6 months of results and re-running headline metrics. If early-window performance is much higher or lower than the rest of the sample, the warm-up issue is material. Suggested fix: Add a sensitivity test: "Excluding the first 26 weeks of backtest to allow BETA6M warm-up, annualized return is [X]% (vs 9.1% full sample), suggesting warm-up bias is [small / moderate / large]."

4. **"Weekday effect is pronounced" — Missing paper attribution for causality.**
   - The draft says: *"This is consistent with the timing of earnings and economic-data releases, which cluster late in the week."* But does the draft or the paper prove causality, or is this correlation? The paper (page 9) argues: *"The premise of mean reversion is that prices tend to revert following overreactions to news. Therefore, on days when we typically experience a higher volume of important news flow, we would also expect to see a stronger mean reversion effect."* The paper provides supporting charts showing earnings and economic-data release timing (page 9, two charts labeled "Percentage of US economic data released on different days" and "Percentage of earnings releases on different days"). The draft cites the intuition but does NOT cite the supporting charts or the paper's explicit argument. Suggested fix: Quote the paper's causality argument and cite the data-release charts.

5. **"Turnover (58.6% 2-way weekly) is substantially lower than the basic R1W reversal baseline" — Paper's baseline is 212%, but what is this run's R1W baseline?**
   - The draft cites the paper's 212% (basic R1W) as a comparison but doesn't compute this run's R1W baseline. If this run's R1W turnover is, say, 300%, the claim that "ML achieves lower turnover" is weaker. Suggested fix: Add a table row for "R1W reversal baseline (this run)" and show the turnover comparison in-sample.

6. **"Analyst rating revisions may lag or lead EPS revisions" — Unsupported claim.**
   - The draft speculates about temporal dynamics of rating vs. EPS revisions but provides no data. A reader might wonder: do ratings actually lag, or lead, or are they synchronous? This should either be cited from research or flagged as an assumption. Suggested fix: Either cite a reference showing rating-lag EPS-revision dynamics, or reframe as: "Rating revisions may have different lag/lead dynamics vs. EPS revisions; this could explain UPDOWN1W_RATINGS' lower rank."

7. **"Market-impact charges beyond the linear bps component... likely understate friction by 20–40 bps annualized" — Unjustified range.**
   - The Limitations section claims unmodeled market impact understates friction by "20–40 bps" but provides no derivation. A 58.6% weekly turnover strategy might face higher or lower impact charges depending on average position size, liquidity profile, and execution style. Suggested fix: Either cite a reference for the 20–40 bps estimate, or reframe as: "Unmodeled market impact and borrow costs likely understate true friction; sensitivity to these costs is outside the scope of this analysis."
